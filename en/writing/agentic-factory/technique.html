Agentic Factory: industrializing AI agents in the enterprise

August 2026 · 10 min read · Anthony Damotte

Everyone knows how to build an impressive agentic demo. Almost no one knows how to keep it alive in production: fewer than one use case in ten makes it past the pilot stage. This paper describes the wall — and the operating model to get past it: replayable workflows, verification gates, honest measurement, capitalization.

Discuss on LinkedIn
Key takeaways
  • The 2025-2026 paradox: ~8 companies in 10 use GenAI, just as many see no bottom-line impact; fewer than 10% of use cases make it past the pilot (McKinsey); > 40% of agentic projects headed for cancellation by 2027 (Gartner).
  • The cause is neither the technology nor the talent: "AI is an amplifier" (DORA) — it magnifies the organization around it. The demonstrator proves a capability; production demands reliability, and the two do not progress together.
  • The answer rests on four floors: versioned, replayable workflows, systematic verification gates, measurement against a baseline, capitalization that makes every project accelerate the next.
  • Field evidence: several hundred consultants mobilized, 100+ engineers in parallel, ~250,000 lines of code, under 3 hours of clocked delivery per demonstrator — the product of the POD format and preparation, not improvisation.

What those sessions prove is a capability — not yet an industry. Between the demonstrator that convinces at 5 pm and the system that will run at the client's a year from now, there is a wall. This paper is about that wall.

The gap is explained neither by access to models nor by talent. McKinsey names the dividing line: moving "from use cases to business processes, from siloed teams to cross-functional squads, from experimentation to industrialized delivery"[1]. DORA sums it up: "AI is an amplifier" — it magnifies existing strengths and dysfunctions[4].

03 · The wall

Why the demonstrator dies in demo mode

  • Capability ≠ reliability. Agents succeed at a task but collapse when required to succeed eight times in a row (τ-bench)[5]; two years of capability gains have produced only modest reliability gains (Princeton)[6]. The demonstrator lives in "it worked"; production lives in "it works every time, including when everything goes wrong".
  • The know-how stays in people's heads. Active agents in Microsoft 365 grew 15x in one year — yet even among the most advanced professionals, only 26% have documented, replayable agent workflows at team level[7]. Three quarters of agentic know-how lives where the knowledge of star developers used to live.
04 · The answer

The four floors of the factory

Versioned, replayable workflows.

Every recurring task (documenting, migrating, generating tests, reverse-documenting) becomes a written process, versioned, replayed and improved. Anthropic: successful implementations use "simple, composable patterns", not complex frameworks[8]; OpenAI: a single agent first, multi-agent only when necessary[9].

Systematic verification gates.

A written Definition of Done (3-5 criteria) before starting; adversarial review after every high-volume run; layered guardrails — it is their combination that creates resilient agents — with two triggers for human escalation: failure thresholds exceeded, high-risk or irreversible actions[9].

Honest measurement.

Every deliverable evaluated against a no-AI baseline (the /100 score). In production: an eval suite that grows — "20 to 50 simple tasks drawn from real failures make an excellent starting point"[10]. And honesty is part of the measurement: output volume is not a demonstrated speed gain.

Capitalization that compounds.

At the end of each engagement, tooling bricks, method rules and evaluation cases join the common foundation: every project starts higher than the one before. The difference between ten agentic projects and ten times the experience of one project.

05 · The operating model

The POD, and the art of picking your battles

The unit of production: 1 client = 1 use case = 1 POD — an experienced lead, tooled-up consultants, and the client in the loop (the best results come from sessions where they arbitrate live). Real material collected before starting, DoD validated in writing. Microsoft measures the same thing at market scale: organizational factors weigh 2x more than individual aptitude[7].

Migrating a legacy module to a modern stack from its specs"Migrate our entire IT landscape" — scope that cannot be bounded

Reverse-documenting a historical piece of softwareA case dependent on an inaccessible client environment
A business assistant over a bounded corpusA result that cannot be demonstrated in a few minutes
Confidentiality: the synthetic-data unblock

A synthetic dataset reproducing the structure and difficulty of the real case demonstrates the same thing as real data — and unblocks a deal in a week instead of a month. It is often the best recommendation, not a fallback.

06 · The conversion

From demonstrator to decision

  • Say up front that a demonstrator is not production-ready as it stands — industrialization is a project in its own right. That honesty avoids the disappointments that kill trust, and paradoxically helps sell.
  • The demonstrator does not create the need, it accelerates a decision already germinating. Follow-on work materializes where a modernization project already existed.
  • Put the decision meeting in calendars before the demonstration. A debrief "to be rescheduled later" is a lost deal. The factory includes the same-day debrief and the dated readout.
07 · Where to start

Four maturity levels

LevelWhat defines it
1. ArtisanalIndividual, undocumented successes — the state of three quarters of the market
2. TooledWritten, replayable workflows, prepared environment
3. MeasuredSystematic verification gates, evaluation against a baseline
4. CapitalizedA common foundation that grows richer with every project, hybrid human-agent teams

Each level takes months to climb, not years — but none can be skipped. The first step is almost free: take your best agentic success of the year and write it up as a workflow someone else can replay. The day your best success no longer belongs to a person but to the organization, your factory exists. The rest is growth.
