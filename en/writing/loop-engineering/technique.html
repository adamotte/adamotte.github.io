Loop engineering: beyond the prompt

August 2026 · 9 min read · Anthony Damotte

Same model, same prompt, two teams: results that are not even comparable. What separates them is the loop — the system that surrounds, repeats, checks and corrects every call to the model. A four-organ anatomy, production patterns, classic anti-patterns.

Discuss on LinkedIn
Key takeaways
  • Agent capability is exploding (the length of completed tasks doubles every ~7 months — METR) while reliability stagnates (modest gains over 2 years — Princeton). The gap between the two is the working space of loop engineering.
  • A loop has four organs: the harness (tools, layered guardrails), the context (a finite resource with diminishing returns), the memory (what the system learns), the evaluation (20-50 tasks drawn from real failures are enough to start).
  • Four production patterns: adversarial second pass, routing by cognitive load, bounded loop depth, governed fan-out. Four symmetrical anti-patterns, including premature multi-agents.
  • Every production agentic system should have its “loop contract” written down: max depth, token budget, verification gates, escalation conditions, evals.
01 · The observation

Why the loop, and not the model

the reliability curve: 12 metrics, 15 models, 2 years of capability for modest gains (Princeton)[3]; collapse at pass^8 (τ-bench)[4]

In our hackathons, a POD produces a demonstrator in under 3 hours of delivery, scored /100 against a baseline — and it is never the prompt that accounts for that performance: it is the prepared environment, the workflows, the checks. Karpathy named part of the phenomenon “context engineering”[1]; loop engineering widens it to the entire loop.

02 · The anatomy

Four organs

The harness — what the agent can do.

Frugality: a few high-impact tools with carefully crafted descriptions — “small refinements in tool descriptions can yield dramatic improvements”[6]. Simplicity: composable patterns (chaining, routing, parallelization, orchestrator-workers, evaluator-optimizer), no opaque frameworks[7]. Layered defense: combined guardrails + two triggers for human escalation — failure threshold, high-risk or irreversible action[8].

The context — what the agent knows right now.

“A finite resource with diminishing marginal returns”[9]. An economic constraint as much as a cognitive one: loop cost is dominated by input tokens — the “snowball” of context reread at every iteration, up to 1000× a chat on coding tasks (Stanford)[10]. Permanently deciding what enters, gets summarized, gets reread on demand — or never enters at all.

The memory — what the system learns.

Self-enforcing project rules, conventions, decision registers: every production lesson ends up encoded somewhere, not in the memory of a person — or of an agent. The most neglected organ and the most profitable: the one that makes a loop improve with use.

The evaluation — what says whether it's good.

Without it, the loop produces the plausible with confidence — the common root cause of failing AI products[11]. To start: “20-50 tasks drawn from real failures”; three families of graders — code, model, human[12]. Criteria emerge from use (“criteria drift”)[13]: the eval is an asset you forge, not something found off the shelf.

03 · The patterns

What keeps the loop standing in production

The adversarial second pass.

After every high-volume production run, a contradictory review cross-checks the strong claims against their sources. Planned into the workflow, not as an afterthought — it is what intercepts overclaims before they reach the client.

Routing by cognitive load.

Budget model for the mechanical, mid-tier for reading/drafting, the most capable for high-stakes judgment calls. Rational: token volume explains 80% of the variance in performance[14] — you place your tokens where they produce.

Bounded loop depth.

METR's horizons provide the rule: beyond the duration the model sustains at 50%, you do not pray — you decompose, you place a verification gate, or you escalate to a human[2].

Governed fan-out.

Massive parallelization for homogeneous batches with low unit stakes; ordered sequencing for critical or interdependent batches. Never the other way around.

04 · The anti-patterns

What derails it

  • The confident infinite loop — no depth bound, no failure threshold: the agent presses on with assurance into a dead end.
  • The snowball context — the window swells, cost and quality degrade together.
  • Premature multi-agents — orchestration complexity before exhausting the single, well-harnessed agent; Anthropic and OpenAI give the same advice: start simple[7][8].
  • Evaluation postponed — steering by impressions, when impressions lie: seasoned developers believe they are 20% faster when they are in fact 19% slower (METR, randomized trial)[15].
05 · In production

The loop contract

To write for every production agentic system

Maximum loop depth · token budget · verification gates and their placement · escalation conditions to a human · evaluation suite and its cadence. A system whose contract you cannot write is not ready for production.

For the framework: “strengthen your safety nets” is one of the seven capabilities of the DORA model that make AI amplify the best rather than the worst[5]; and NIST's GenAI profile provides the full grammar for formalizing (12 risk categories, 200+ actions)[16].

And tomorrow? More reliable models will lighten the loop — so much the better. But it will not disappear: it encodes what the model will never know on its own — what your organization calls a job well done, what it forbids, what it has learned from its failures. A prompt can be copied in ten seconds; a loop has to be built. That is precisely why it is worth something.
